THE ENTRANCE

Why this coast cooks on embers

In a land of few trees, fire was never something to waste. What burned once had to feed twice — and so this coast learned to wait for the fire to settle.

Flame is in a hurry: it scorches the surface, leaves the middle raw, and gives soot instead of taste. The ember is the fire after it has calmed — clean steady heat that cooks from within, and gives rice a golden floor no stove can make.

Between flame and ash there is one hour.
That hour is the whole kitchen.

And here the ember is not only under the meat: the dallah sits on its coals until the coffee is ready, and oud is laid on an ember to welcome a guest. One ember cooks, serves and perfumes.

And from that hour we took the name. Ala Jamr
